Boldenone (Equipoise): What the Veterinary Steroid Is, Its Legal Status and Health Risks

Boldenone (Equipoise) is an FDA-approved horse steroid, Schedule III in the US and banned by WADA. What the law says and the health risks of misuse.

GBNT Newsroom • Published Nov 15, 2026 • 5 min read
Share

Boldenone undecylenate, sold under the brand name Equipoise, is an anabolic steroid approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) only for use in horses, on a veterinarian's order. In the United States boldenone is a Schedule III controlled substance, it is banned at all times in sport under the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) rules, and federal health agencies link anabolic steroid misuse to heart, liver, hormonal and mental health harm.

Boldenone (Equipoise), the key facts:

  • What it is: an injectable anabolic steroid, a chemical relative of testosterone, marketed for veterinary use as Equipoise.
  • Approved use: horses only, "as an aid for treating debilitated horses," under FDA approval NADA 34-705.
  • US law: listed by name as a Schedule III anabolic steroid in federal drug law and DEA regulations.
  • Sport: prohibited in and out of competition as an S1.1 anabolic androgenic steroid.
  • Health: NIDA links anabolic steroid misuse to heart attacks, strokes, liver damage, infertility and depression.
  • Help: the SAMHSA National Helpline is 1-800-662-HELP (4357).

What Is Boldenone Undecylenate?

Boldenone is an anabolic androgenic steroid. US federal law names it in the legal definition of an "anabolic steroid," a drug or hormonal substance chemically and pharmacologically related to testosterone. The undecylenate part of the name is an ester attached to the molecule, and the product that carries it in the US is Equipoise injection.

A 2023 review in the Journal of Veterinary Pharmacology and Therapeutics notes that anabolic steroids "have been illegally used for many years as performance-enhancing drugs in human, equine, and canine sports and as growth promoters in livestock." The FDA approval on record for Equipoise covers horses, not people.

What Is Equipoise Approved For?

Equipoise is an FDA-approved new animal drug under NADA 34-705. A Federal Register final rule published on November 25, 2005, records that FDA approved a supplemental application from Fort Dodge Animal Health on October 7, 2005, giving the product "a revised indication and food safety warning on labeling."

Under that approval, the drug is indicated "as an aid for treating debilitated horses when an improvement in weight, hair coat, or general physical condition is desired." The label limitations read: "Do not administer to horses intended for human consumption. Federal law restricts this drug to use by or on the order of a licensed veterinarian."

A March 2014 Federal Register rule later moved Equipoise, along with more than 100 other animal drug approvals, from Pfizer to Zoetis, Inc.

Is Boldenone Legal in the United States?

Boldenone is a controlled substance. It is named in the definition of anabolic steroid in the Controlled Substances Act at 21 U.S.C. 802(41), and it appears in the DEA's Schedule III list at 21 CFR 1308.13(f), which covers anabolic steroids "including its salts, esters and ethers" under drug code 4000.

In practice, its only lawful US use is by or on the order of a veterinarian treating a horse. Buying, selling or possessing it outside that system can bring federal and state criminal liability.

Is Boldenone Banned in Sport?

Yes. The International Testing Agency explains that boldenone is in class S1.1 Anabolic Androgenic Steroids on the WADA Prohibited List, is prohibited at all times (in and out of competition) and is "classified as a non-specified substance."

What Are the Health Risks of Anabolic Steroid Misuse?

There are no human clinical trials of boldenone to draw on, so the evidence comes from research on anabolic steroids as a class. The National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA) says anabolic steroids "can cause severe, long-lasting, and in some cases, irreversible damage." Effects it lists include:

  • Heart and blood vessels: heart attacks, artery damage and strokes, "even in athletes younger than 30," plus a higher risk of blood clots.
  • Liver: liver damage, tumors and peliosis hepatis, in which blood-filled cysts form in the liver and can rupture.
  • Hormones: reduced sperm production, testicular shrinkage and breast growth in men; masculinizing changes in women; bones that stop growing too early in teens.
  • Mental health: irritability, aggression, anxiety, and links to mania and major depression.
  • Dependence: NIDA reports that about 32 percent of people who misuse anabolic steroids become dependent, and that depression during withdrawal "sometimes leads to suicide attempts."

In October 2016 the FDA added a class-wide warning to testosterone labels, saying abuse of testosterone and other anabolic androgenic steroids is associated with "serious safety risks affecting the heart, brain, liver, mental health, and endocrine system," including heart attack, heart failure, stroke, liver toxicity and male infertility, as reported by RAPS.

A 2014 Endocrine Society scientific statement in Endocrine Reviews found that performance-enhancing drug use "has been linked to an increased risk of death and a wide variety of cardiovascular, psychiatric, metabolic, endocrine, neurologic, infectious, hepatic, renal, and musculoskeletal disorders," and that most users are "not athletes but rather nonathlete weightlifters."

Is Boldenone a Food Safety Concern?

The FDA label itself bars use in horses intended for human consumption. A 2024 review in Analytical Methods says boldenone "has been frequently detected in doping and food safety over the past few decades," and that detection is complicated by low levels of naturally occurring related compounds.

Where Can Someone Get Help?

Anyone worried about their own or someone else's steroid use can call the SAMHSA National Helpline at 1-800-662-HELP (4357) or visit FindTreatment.gov, both listed by NIDA. In a crisis, call or text 988. NIDA notes that treatment often addresses the reasons behind steroid use, and can include therapy and hormone care from a doctor.

FAQs

Is Equipoise approved for humans?

No. FDA approval covers prescription veterinary use in horses only.

What schedule is boldenone?

Schedule III, as an anabolic steroid named in 21 U.S.C. 802(41) and 21 CFR 1308.13(f).

Can athletes use boldenone out of competition?

No. WADA rules prohibit it at all times, and positive tests have led to four-year bans.
