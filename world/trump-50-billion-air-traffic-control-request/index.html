Trump Administration Seeks $50 Billion for Air Traffic Control After Nationwide Ground Stop

The White House raised its air traffic control request to $50 billion from $30.1 billion after a ground stop; Democrats ask how 2025's $12.5 billion was spent.

GBNT Newsroom • Published Nov 16, 2026 • 5 min read
Share

The Trump administration raised its air traffic control funding request to $50 billion late on Friday, November 13, 2026, hours after a communications failure forced a rare nationwide ground stop that halted thousands of flights. Earlier that same day, the White House and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy had asked Congress for $30.1 billion. President Trump followed up on Sunday, November 15, urging lawmakers to approve the money quickly, while Democrats asked how earlier funding has been spent.

The $50 billion air traffic control request, the key facts:

  • New ask: $50 billion, up from a $30.1 billion request sent to Congress on November 13, Reuters reported.
  • Already approved: Congress approved $12.5 billion over five years in July 2025 for air traffic control upgrades and controller hiring.
  • Trigger: A nationwide ground stop of around four hours on Friday, tied to a telecommunications problem.
  • What Duffy wants: "new hardware, new software, new telecommunication," he said at a Friday night briefing.
  • Politics: The administration wants action in the final weeks of the year, before Democrats take control of Congress in January.

What Did the Administration Ask For, and How Did It Grow?

On Friday, the White House and Duffy sent Congress a $30.1 billion request to upgrade the country's airports and air traffic control system, Reuters reported. After the ground stop, the administration boosted the figure to $50 billion later that night.

"This is an emergency," Duffy said, according to Reuters. "What's more important than having an air traffic control system that actually works?"

At a Friday night briefing, Duffy argued against piecemeal repairs. "Let's not do a patchwork fix to a system that's critical for every single family," he said, The Hill reported. "Let's finish this job: new hardware, new software, new telecommunication."

The administration has not yet published a line-by-line breakdown of the full $50 billion. Duffy said he would send lawmakers a detailed request on Monday, the Associated Press reported.

What Would the Money Buy?

The itemized $30.1 billion request gives the clearest picture so far. According to Reuters, it includes the following:

ItemAmount
Air traffic control modernization (total)$10.1 billion
Of which: common automation platform and controller workstations$7.2 billion
Of which: cloud computing$2 billion
Replacing and improving FAA control towers$2 billion
Airport-related projects (including $10 billion for aging terminals)$18 billion

Duffy has said the larger request would pay for a "brand new architecture" for the telecommunications network, and that carriers such as AT&T, Verizon and Lumen could deploy it "tomorrow" if the money were available, AP reported.

The effort builds on the plan Duffy unveiled in May 2025. That plan, published by the FAA, calls for replacing 618 radars that have gone past their life cycle, installing new fiber, wireless and satellite telecommunications at more than 4,600 sites, adding 25,000 new radios and 475 new voice switches, and building six new air traffic control centers for the first time since the 1960s. In the letter, the administration said the FAA has replaced 68% of its legacy copper telecommunications network with fiber, Reuters reported.

What Caused the Ground Stop?

Duffy said the problem began when a federal contractor replaced old copper wire with fiber, overloading the system, according to AP. The FAA said "excessive data" was flooding the network, and blocking the two affected locations stabilized it. Duffy said there were no signs of a cyberattack.

Duffy said flights should be back to full capacity by Monday, AP reported.

How Is Congress Reacting?

Democrats say they support upgrading the system but want answers first. House Transportation Committee Democrats said most of the 2025 money remains unspent, AP reported. "Explain to Congress, again, why they need more money?" Reps. Rick Larsen and Andre Carson said in a joint statement. Rep. Rosa DeLauro, the top Democrat on the House Appropriations Committee, said "we need a competent partner at the Department of Transportation to carry this out."

Duffy said the 2025 money will be spent by the end of 2028 and that Congress restricted how it could be used in ways that excluded telecommunications infrastructure, according to AP. House Speaker Mike Johnson, a Republican, said "we need further investment," The Hill reported.

Trump weighed in on Truth Social on Sunday. "We have already started the work, but it should be done all at once, not in drips and drabs," he wrote. "We have the knowledge and plan for the Flight Control Systems, we need Congress to give the money, FAST!"

Why Does It Matter for Airlines, Travelers and Business?

The U.S. handles roughly 45,000 flights a day, and a single network fault on Friday stopped departures nationwide for hours. Repeated outages ripple through airline schedules, crew rotations, cargo and business travel. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford has told lawmakers that a second modernization phase would cost at least $10 billion more, and Sen. Eric Schmitt has introduced a bill calling for about $30 billion in new investment, AP reported.

What Happens Next?

The detailed request is expected to reach lawmakers this week. The administration wants Congress to act in the final weeks of 2026, before Democrats take control in January. The first phase of the modernization, funded by the $12.5 billion, is due to be completed by December 2028.

FAQs

How much is the Trump administration asking for air traffic control?

The administration is seeking $50 billion, up from a $30.1 billion request sent to Congress earlier on November 13, 2026, according to Reuters.

Didn't Congress already fund air traffic control upgrades?

Yes. Congress approved $12.5 billion over five years in July 2025. Duffy says that money will be spent by the end of 2028, while Democrats say most of it remains unspent.

Is the $50 billion approved?

No. It is a request to Congress, and no vote had been reported as of November 16, 2026.
