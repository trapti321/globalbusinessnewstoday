Electronic Learning (E-Learning): Types, Evidence, LMS Choice and Accessibility Rules

Electronic learning explained: types of e-learning, what the evidence says, how firms choose an LMS, and WCAG, Section 508 and ADA rules.

GBNT Newsroom • Published Nov 15, 2026 • 5 min read
Share

Electronic learning, usually shortened to e-learning and also called electronic education, means teaching and training delivered through digital devices and networks, from live video classes to self-paced courses inside a company learning platform. It is now mainstream: in fall 2021, 61% of US undergraduates took at least one distance education course. The research says it can work as well as classroom teaching, and that it works best when it is blended with face-to-face instruction.

Electronic learning, the key facts:

  • What it is: education or training delivered online or through software, live or self-paced.
  • US college reach: 9.4 million undergraduates (61%) took at least one distance course in fall 2021, and 4.4 million (28%) studied fully online (NCES).
  • Evidence: a US Department of Education meta-analysis found online learners did "modestly better" on average than face-to-face learners, with the biggest gains in blended setups.
  • Business scale: Coursera reported 197 million registered learners at the end of 2025 and completed its all-stock combination with Udemy on May 11, 2026.
  • Accessibility: Section 508 applies WCAG 2.0 Level AA to federal electronic content. The ADA Title II rule requires WCAG 2.1 AA for public schools, colleges and universities, with first deadlines in April 2027.

What Is Electronic Learning?

Electronic learning is any structured learning where the content, the teaching or the assessment reaches the learner through a computer, phone or network. "Electronic education" and "online learning" are generally used to mean the same thing, while "distance education" is the term US federal statistics use.

Does Electronic Learning Actually Work?

The most cited evidence is a meta-analysis by SRI International for the US Department of Education (revised September 2010). Researchers screened more than a thousand studies published from 1996 to July 2008 and kept 50 effects that met strict criteria. The findings:

  • Students in online conditions performed modestly better on average, with an average effect size of +0.20.
  • Blended instruction showed the larger advantage over face-to-face teaching (+0.35). Purely online versus purely face-to-face was +0.05, which was not statistically significant.
  • The authors warned that blended groups often got extra learning time and materials, so the gains "should not be attributed to the media, per se."
  • Few rigorous K-12 studies existed, so the results come mostly from higher education and professional training such as medicine.

How Big Is Online Learning in the US?

According to the National Center for Education Statistics, the share of undergraduates taking at least one distance course went from 36% in 2019 to 75% in 2020 (the pandemic year) and settled at 61% in 2021. Among postbaccalaureate students, 56% took at least one distance course in 2021 and 40% studied exclusively at a distance.

Access remains a global limit. UNESCO says only 40% of primary schools and 50% of lower secondary schools worldwide are connected to the internet.

What Are the Benefits and Limits?

Benefits: flexible timing, reach across locations, consistent content for every learner, and data on completion and quiz results. Limits: it depends on devices and connectivity, self-paced courses need learner discipline, and hands-on or social skills often still need in-person practice.

Why Does E-Learning Matter for Business?

Corporate training is a major user. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics counts about 472,500 training and development specialist jobs (median pay $69,280 in May 2025), projects 11% growth from 2025 to 2035, and notes that these specialists build online learning modules.

LinkedIn's 2025 Workplace Learning Report (937 L&D and HR professionals and 679 learners surveyed) found that 49% say their executives worry employees lack the skills to carry out business strategy, and 88% of organizations are concerned about retention.

On the platform side, Coursera reported 2025 revenue of $757.5 million (up 9%), enterprise revenue of $255.3 million and 1,730 paid enterprise customers. Its quarterly SEC filing confirms that the combination with Udemy closed on May 11, 2026. We did not find a market-size estimate for e-learning from a source we could check, so we have left out the headline "market worth" figures that often circulate.

How Should a Company or SME Choose an LMS?

  • Start with the use case: compliance tracking, onboarding, customer training or skills development each need different features.
  • Check standards support: ask which course package formats the LMS imports and exports, so your content is not locked to one vendor.
  • Reporting: make sure managers can see completions, overdue training and audit records.
  • Accessibility: ask for a conformance report against WCAG.
  • Total cost: compare per-user pricing, content libraries, setup fees and contract length, and run a pilot before you sign.

What Accessibility Rules Apply to E-Learning?

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are the reference standard. W3C recommends the latest version, WCAG 2.2 (published October 5, 2023), and content that meets 2.2 also meets 2.1 and 2.0. In the US:

  • Section 508 applies WCAG 2.0 Level AA to federal agencies' web and non-web electronic content.
  • ADA Title II: the Justice Department's web rule requires state and local governments, including public schools, community colleges and public universities, to meet WCAG 2.1 AA. After an April 2026 extension, deadlines are April 26, 2027 for entities serving 50,000 or more people and April 26, 2028 for smaller ones.

In practice: captions, keyboard navigation, good color contrast and screen-reader-friendly quizzes.

FAQs

Is electronic learning the same as electronic education?

Yes, in everyday use. Both mean learning delivered through digital technology. US government statistics generally say "distance education."

Is online learning as effective as classroom learning?

The Department of Education meta-analysis found online learners did modestly better on average, but the clearest gains came from blended courses, and the authors linked those gains to extra time and materials rather than the technology itself.

Do e-learning courses have to be accessible?

For US federal agencies (Section 508) and public schools and universities (ADA Title II), yes. Private companies should treat WCAG 2.1 or 2.2 Level AA as the practical benchmark.
