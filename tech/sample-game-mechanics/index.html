Sample Game Mechanics: Real Examples From Tetris, Mario, Minecraft, Chess and Magic

Sample game mechanics explained with real examples, a table of common mechanics, the MDA framework, design docs and loot box rules.

GBNT Newsroom • Published Nov 15, 2026 • 5 min read
Share

Game mechanics are the rules and systems a player acts through: jumping, drawing a card, clearing a line, crafting an item or opening a loot box. Good sample game mechanics come straight from real games, such as Tetris line clears, chess castling and the Magic: The Gathering draw step. Designers write them up in a game design document, and regulators now watch one in particular: paid random rewards.

Sample game mechanics, the key facts:

  • Definition (MDA framework): mechanics are "the particular components of the game, at the level of data representation and algorithms."
  • Classic examples: Tetris line clears (1984), Mario's jump (Super Mario Bros., 1985), chess castling and en passant, the Magic: The Gathering draw step.
  • Regulation: the FTC's $20 million Genshin Impact settlement (January 2025) targeted loot box odds and sales to under-16s. Belgium's regulator found loot boxes in three games broke its gambling law in 2018.

What Are Game Mechanics?

The most widely taught definition comes from the MDA paper by Robin Hunicke, Marc LeBlanc and Robert Zubek, published in the 2004 AAAI workshop proceedings and developed in the Game Design and Tuning Workshop at the Game Developers Conference (2001 to 2004). The paper splits a game into three layers:

  • Mechanics: the components of the game "at the level of data representation and algorithms" (the rules).
  • Dynamics: "the run-time behavior of the mechanics acting on player inputs and each others outputs over time."
  • Aesthetics: "the desirable emotional responses evoked in the player."

The authors list eight kinds of aesthetic: Sensation, Fantasy, Narrative, Challenge, Fellowship, Discovery, Expression and Submission. They note that designers work from mechanics up, while players experience aesthetics first and reach the mechanics last.

What Are Some Sample Game Mechanics?

Tetris: line clears

Tetris was created by programmer Alexey Pajitnov in 1984, according to The Tetris Company. The core mechanic: complete a horizontal row of blocks with no empty space and the row clears. Pieces fall faster as you progress, and the game ends when the stack goes past the top.

Super Mario Bros.: jumping

Nintendo lists Super Mario Bros. as released on September 13, 1985, for the Nintendo Entertainment System. Jumping is the main action in the game: it is how you get over gaps, reach platforms and defeat enemies by landing on them.

Minecraft: crafting

In Minecraft, items combine into new items using recipes. Minecraft's official guide explains that a crafting table takes four planks, that the table unlocks all crafting recipes, and that the recipe book can be opened from your inventory or at a crafting table.

Chess: special moves

The FIDE Laws of Chess (in force since January 1, 2023) say White moves first and players alternate turns. The aim is checkmate. Castling moves the king and a rook in one turn, and the en passant capture is legal only on the move right after an opposing pawn advances two squares. The 50-move rule lets a player claim a draw after 50 moves by each side with no pawn move or capture.

Magic: The Gathering: card draw

Under the Comprehensive Rules (effective November 6, 2026), each player starts with 20 life and a seven-card hand, can take a mulligan, and "first, the active player draws a card" each turn. A player who has to draw from an empty library loses.

How Do Designers Document Game Mechanics?

Studios usually record mechanics in a game design document (GDD), a living reference the whole team works from. A useful entry for each mechanic includes:

  • The rule in plain words and the player input that triggers it.
  • Numbers: costs, timers, drop chances, damage or score values.
  • Edge cases: ties, empty decks, timing windows (the equivalent of chess's en passant).
  • The intended dynamic and aesthetic (MDA terms).
  • A worked example showing the start state, the action and the result step by step.

That last point is also the main advice in a widely shared explainer on game mechanic examples: show the starting state, every legal option and the calculations, and mark which outcomes depend on chance.

What Is the Business Side: Gamification and Loot Box Rules?

Gamification means using game mechanics such as points, levels, badges, leaderboards and progress bars in work, training or apps. As MDA suggests, copying a mechanic does not guarantee the intended experience, so test it.

Loot boxes are the mechanic facing the most legal scrutiny:

  • US (FTC): in January 2025, Genshin Impact publisher HoYoverse (Cognosphere) agreed to a $20 million penalty over allegations that it misled players about prize odds and costs. The proposed order bans loot box sales to under-16s without parental consent and requires odds disclosure. Separately, Epic Games agreed in 2022 to $520 million in total over alleged COPPA violations and confusing purchase design in Fortnite.
  • Ratings (ESRB): since April 2020, the "In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items)" notice flags loot boxes, gacha, card packs, prize wheels and similar purchases.
  • Belgium: in April 2018, the Belgian Gaming Commission found that loot boxes in Overwatch, FIFA 18 and Counter-Strike: Global Offensive violated gambling law, Game Informer reported.
  • UK: the government's July 2022 response chose not to bring loot boxes under the Gambling Act 2005. It said they should be unavailable to children unless a parent or guardian enables them, and that industry should improve age checks and spending controls.

FAQs

What is a simple example of a game mechanic?

The Tetris line clear: fill a horizontal row completely and it disappears.

What is the difference between mechanics and dynamics?

In the MDA framework, mechanics are the rules, while dynamics are what happens when those rules meet player choices over time, such as the tension of a stack rising in Tetris.

Are loot boxes gambling?

It depends on the country. Belgium's regulator found some loot boxes broke its gambling law in 2018. The UK chose not to classify them as gambling, and US action so far has come through FTC consumer protection cases.
