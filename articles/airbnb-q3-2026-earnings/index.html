Airbnb Q3 2026 Earnings: Revenue Up 19%, Outlook Raised and $12 Billion Buyback

Airbnb grew Q3 2026 revenue 19% to $4.9 billion, raised its full-year outlook and approved a $12 billion buyback. Here is what drove it and what is next.

GBNT Newsroom • Published Nov 9, 2026 • 5 min read
Share

Airbnb's revenue grew 19% to $4.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026, its fastest growth in more than three years, and the company raised its full-year outlook and approved up to $12 billion more in share buybacks. The results, released on November 5, 2026, show its push into services, hotels and AI support starting to add up.

Airbnb Q3 2026 at a glance:

  • Revenue: $4.9 billion, up 19% year over year
  • Gross booking value: $27.7 billion, up 21%
  • Nights and seats booked: up 16%
  • Net income: $1.9 billion
  • Adjusted EBITDA: $2.5 billion, up 22%, a 52% margin
  • New buyback: up to an additional $12 billion

What Airbnb Reported

In its third-quarter results, Airbnb said revenue rose 19% from a year earlier to $4.9 billion. Gross booking value, the total value of stays and services booked on the platform, rose 21% to $27.7 billion. Bookings grew faster than the number of nights and seats sold (up 16%), so the average value of each booking went up.

Profitability held up. Net income was $1.9 billion. Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation, excluding certain items) rose 22% to $2.5 billion, a 52% margin, reflecting the summer travel peak.

Earnings per share of $3.16 beat the $2.85 analysts expected, and the shares rose about 11% in after-hours trading, CNBC reported. That came during a volatile week for markets, covered in our report on the Nasdaq's correction and rebound.

Airbnb Raises Its 2026 Outlook

  • Full-year revenue growth: raised to the high teens.
  • Full-year adjusted EBITDA margin: raised to about 36%.
  • Fourth-quarter revenue: $3.22 billion to $3.28 billion, up 16% to 18%, including about two percentage points from favourable exchange rates.
  • Fourth-quarter gross booking value: mid-teens growth.
  • Fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA: expected to grow, with a margin slightly below the fourth quarter of 2025.

Where the Growth Is Coming From

Services beyond stays

Airbnb is selling more than places to sleep. On September 30 it added meal delivery, laundry, baby gear, ski gear and boat rentals to its services. Grocery delivery has expanded across the US and Canada and recently launched in Europe. Car rentals, launched over the summer, have become one of its most popular services, the company said.

Hotels and partnerships

Thousands of boutique and independent hotels are now listed in more than 60 top destinations. An exclusive partnership with American Express, announced on October 22, will bring more than one million Airbnb listings to Amex Travel.

AI customer support

Airbnb's AI support assistant now works in more than 50 languages. About 40% of customer support issues were resolved without a human agent in the third quarter, up from a third in the second quarter. An AI trip-planning agent is due to roll out to guests in the first half of 2027. For how other companies are using AI to cut costs, see our report on enterprise AI adoption and ROI.

What It Means for Hosts and Small Businesses

  • More demand, more competition: faster booking growth helps hosts, but more hotels on the platform means more competition for guests in big cities.
  • New income streams: the services marketplace opens Airbnb to local businesses such as caterers, cleaners and equipment rental firms.
  • Less human support: with more issues handled by AI, hosts with complex disputes may need to ask specifically for a human agent.

Airbnb also said every stay now funds a $0.10 donation to Airbnb.org, aiming to provide $500 million over five years.

Airbnb Q3 2026 FAQs

How much revenue did Airbnb make in Q3 2026?

$4.9 billion, up 19% from a year earlier.

Did Airbnb raise its guidance?

Yes. It now expects full-year 2026 revenue growth in the high teens and an adjusted EBITDA margin of about 36%.

How big is Airbnb's new buyback?

Up to an additional $12 billion of Class A shares.
