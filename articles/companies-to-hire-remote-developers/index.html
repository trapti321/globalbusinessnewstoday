Best Companies to Hire Remote Developers in 2026: 8 Options Compared

Toptal, Arc, Andela, Upwork, Deel and more: how 8 ways to hire remote developers compare on vetting, speed, cost and who carries the risk.

GBNT Newsroom • Published Nov 4, 2026 • 8 min read
Share

Choosing a company to hire remote developers from is less about finding the "best" platform than about matching a hiring model to the job. A vetted freelance network, an open marketplace, a nearshore agency and an employer of record each shift cost, speed and risk in different ways.

This guide compares eight well-known options. Each is grouped by model and checked against the provider's own published terms on November 4, 2026. Network sizes, acceptance rates and matching times are company-reported marketing claims, and we label them that way. None of the companies paid to appear here.

Why companies keep hiring developers remotely

Demand for engineers remains strong. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects software developer employment to grow 10% from 2025 to 2035, adding about 174,700 jobs. It puts the median US salary at $135,980 in May 2025. That cost is a big reason companies look further afield.

Developers are used to working this way. In the 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey of more than 49,000 developers, 32.4% said they worked fully remotely and 17.9% fully in person. The rest worked hybrid or chose freely when to come in.

The four ways to hire remote developers

  • Vetted talent networks pre-screen developers and match you quickly. They cost more per hour but carry less mis-hire risk.
  • Open marketplaces give you the widest choice at the lowest headline rates, but screening is your job.
  • Managed or nearshore teams supply a whole squad and often take responsibility for delivery.
  • Employers of record (EOR) don't find talent. They legally employ the person you have chosen in their own country and handle payroll, benefits and compliance.

Vetted talent networks

1. Toptal

Toptal is the best-known premium network. On its website it says it hires "the top 3% of freelance talent" and has 30,000+ talent in its network. It says the average time to match is under 24 hours. Clients start on a trial basis and "pay only if satisfied", and Toptal reports a 98% trial-to-hire success rate. Rates sit at the top of the market.

Best for: senior specialists needed quickly, where the budget allows.

2. Arc

Arc lists 450,000 talent in 190 countries. It says its freelance contractors are the "top 2%" and fully vetted for domain expertise and English fluency. It targets freelance hires within 72 hours and full-time hires within 14 days. Employers pay nothing until they hire.

Best for: companies that want both contract and permanent hires through one platform.

3. Lemon.io

Lemon.io focuses on startups. It reports a 1.2% acceptance rate and a matching time of about 24 hours, with developers from Europe, Latin America, the US and Canada. Hours are prepaid, typically a month ahead, with a 160-hour minimum and no recruitment fee.

Best for: early-stage teams that need one or two experienced engineers without running a hiring process.

Managed teams and nearshore partners

4. Andela

Andela began by training engineers in Africa and now pitches itself around AI-era engineering. It cites 17,000 "certified AI-native engineers", 200,000+ talent trained on emerging technologies and 650+ enterprise clients. As well as placing talent, it offers skills assessment and managed delivery of AI systems.

Best for: larger companies building distributed teams that also need upskilling.

5. BairesDev

BairesDev was founded in 2009 and focuses on nearshore development for North American clients. Its website advertises 4,000+ "timezone aligned" software engineers and says more than 500 companies rely on its "top 1%" talent. The main advantage over Asian offshore teams is working-hour overlap with the US.

Best for: US companies that want real-time collaboration and a provider that can staff a whole project.

6. X-Team

Australian-registered X-Team points to a 20-year history and a 98% talent retention rate. It offers four engagement models: staff augmentation, permanent placement, recruitment process outsourcing (RPO), and project-based squads that own delivery.

Best for: long engagements where keeping the same developers matters.

Open marketplace

7. Upwork

Upwork is one of the largest general freelance marketplaces. It offers search, escrow-style payment protection, contracts and public reviews, but vetting is largely up to you. Clients pay a marketplace fee on top of the freelancer's rate, plus a small fee for each new contract. Check the current rates on Upwork's client pricing page, because they vary by plan and payment method.

Best for: well-defined, short projects where you can judge code quality yourself.

Employer of record

8. Deel and Remote

If you have already found a developer abroad and want to hire them full time without opening a local company, an EOR is the usual route.

  • Deel says it covers 150+ countries and handles contracts, payroll, benefits, terminations and IP transfer clauses.
  • Remote covers more than 90 countries and publishes pricing from $599 per employee per month on annual contracts. It says it owns all its entities.

Best for: turning a trusted contractor into a compliant full-time employee.

Comparison at a glance

  • Fastest match: Toptal and Lemon.io (about 24 hours, company-reported).
  • Lowest headline cost: Upwork, though screening time is a real cost.
  • Best US time-zone overlap: nearshore Latin American providers such as BairesDev.
  • Full-time employment abroad: an EOR such as Deel or Remote.
  • Delivery accountability: managed squads from Andela, BairesDev or X-Team.

How to choose: seven checks before you sign

  1. Decide whether you need a person or an outcome. Without an engineering lead in-house, buy a managed team, not individuals.
  2. Ask how vetting works. "Top 1%" claims are marketing. Ask what the technical test involves and who runs it.
  3. Run a paid trial. One or two weeks on real work tells you more than any interview.
  4. Check time-zone overlap. Aim for at least a few working hours shared every day.
  5. Price the total cost. Add platform fees, management time, onboarding and rework to the hourly rate.
  6. Confirm IP and security terms. Make sure code ownership transfers to you, and limit who has admin access to production systems.
  7. Ask about AI coding tools. Find out how the provider reviews and tests AI-generated code before it reaches your repository. For the bigger picture, see our report on enterprise AI adoption and ROI.
