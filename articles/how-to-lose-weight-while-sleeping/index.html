How Do We Lose Weight While Sleeping? Water, Fat and What Sleep Really Does

How do we lose weight while sleeping? Mostly water lost through breath and sweat, not fat. Here is what studies show about sleep, hunger and weight.

GBNT Newsroom • Published Nov 12, 2026 • 5 min read
Share

We lose weight while sleeping mainly through water: moisture in every breath, sweat through the skin, and urine passed when we wake. A small amount of stored fuel, including some fat, is also burned to keep the body running, but that is a few hundred calories at most. Sleep matters for real fat loss because of what it does to hunger and eating the next day.

Losing weight while sleeping, the key facts:

  • The overnight scale drop is mostly water lost through breathing and sweat, not fat.
  • Your body still burns calories asleep, but sleeping metabolic rate is lower than when you are awake and resting.
  • Fat leaves mostly through your lungs: about 84% of the mass of burned fat is breathed out as carbon dioxide.
  • Sleeping more can cut eating: in a 2022 trial, adults who slept about 1.2 hours longer ate about 270 fewer calories a day.
  • Short sleep can undermine a diet: dieters given 5.5 hours in bed lost less fat and more lean mass than with 8.5 hours.

Do You Lose Weight When You Sleep?

Yes, most people weigh a little less in the morning, but most of that loss is water. Every breath carries out water vapor, and the skin loses moisture through sweat. You also do not eat or drink for 7 to 9 hours.

Breathing losses alone are large enough to measure. In a 1995 study, 10 adults slept in tents below freezing. Wearing a mask that trapped breath moisture cut their overnight weight loss by an average of 0.13 kg, or about a quarter pound.

That water comes back as soon as you drink, so one night's drop says little about fat.

How Do We Lose Weight While Sleeping, Exactly?

Through three routes: water lost from breath and skin, urine, and carbon dioxide from burning fuel. Your heart, lungs, brain and cells keep working all night, and that energy comes from glucose and fat.

When fat is burned, it does not turn into heat or muscle. A widely cited 2014 BMJ analysis worked out that about 84% of the mass of burned fat leaves as carbon dioxide through the lungs. The other 16% becomes water.

How Many Calories Do You Burn While Sleeping?

Fewer than when you are awake and resting. In a Cambridge study of six adults, sleeping metabolic rate was about 12% below basal metabolic rate. Their sleeping rate worked out to roughly 50 calories an hour, or about 400 over 8 hours (our calculation).

The Sleep Foundation gives a similar picture, saying metabolism slows about 15% during sleep. Larger, more muscular people burn more. Either way, sleep is not a fat burning shortcut.

Can Sleep Help You Lose Weight?

Yes, mostly by changing how much you eat. In a 2022 JAMA Internal Medicine trial, 80 adults with overweight who usually slept under 6.5 hours got one sleep coaching session. They slept about 1.2 hours more a night and ate about 270 fewer calories a day than the control group, with no diet prescribed.

Sleep also shapes what you lose. In a 2010 Annals of Internal Medicine study, 10 overweight adults dieted for 14 days twice, once with 8.5 hours in bed and once with 5.5 hours. With short sleep they lost 0.6 kg of fat instead of 1.4 kg, and more lean mass, and they felt hungrier.

Hunger hormones help explain it. In a 2004 study of 12 young men, two nights of short sleep cut the fullness hormone leptin by 18% and raised the hunger hormone ghrelin by 28%.

Does Sleeping in a Cold Room Burn Fat?

Not in a way that shows on the scale. In a 2014 NIH study, five men slept at 66°F (19°C) for a month. Their brown fat increased and insulin sensitivity improved, but body composition did not change. For more on cold and calories, read whether cold showers make you lose weight.

What to Do Tonight

  1. Aim for 7 or more hours. The CDC recommends at least 7 hours for adults aged 18 to 60.
  2. Keep a steady schedule. Go to bed and wake at similar times, including weekends.
  3. Plan for next-day hunger. After a short night, have protein-rich meals ready so cravings do not decide for you.
  4. Weigh yourself the same way each time. Morning, after using the bathroom, before eating, and judge the weekly trend.
  5. Pair sleep with the basics. A modest calorie deficit and regular activity, such as running or walking, still do the heavy lifting.
  6. See a doctor about loud snoring or daytime sleepiness. They can be signs of sleep apnea, which is treatable.

FAQs

Why do I weigh less in the morning?

Mainly because you lost water through breathing and sweat overnight and have not eaten or drunk anything yet. It is not a sign of fat loss.

Can you lose fat while you sleep?

Your body does burn some fat overnight, but only as part of a few hundred calories of normal energy use. Fat loss over weeks depends on your total calorie balance.

Does lack of sleep cause weight gain?

Short sleep raises hunger and can increase eating, which makes weight gain more likely. Trials show sleeping longer can reduce daily calorie intake.

How many hours of sleep do I need to lose weight?

There is no magic number, but the CDC advises at least 7 hours for adults. The 2022 trial cut calorie intake by extending short sleep by about an hour.
