Shopify vs WooCommerce in 2026: Real Costs, Fees and Which to Choose

Shopify vs WooCommerce compared on 2026 pricing, card fees, security and control, with a worked cost example and a quick guide on which to choose.

GBNT Newsroom • Published Nov 10, 2026 • 9 min read
Share

Shopify vs WooCommerce comes down to one trade-off: Shopify charges a monthly fee and runs the store for you, while WooCommerce is free software that you host, secure and maintain yourself. For most small businesses selling online, the platform fee is the smallest part of the bill. Card processing costs about the same on both, so the real decision is how much time, control and technical risk you want to take on. We compared both platforms' official pricing, fee schedules and security documentation on November 10, 2026.

Shopify vs WooCommerce, the short answer:

  • Choose Shopify if you want a store running this week, sell in person as well as online, or have no one to manage hosting, updates and security.
  • Choose WooCommerce if you already run a WordPress site, want full control of your data and code, or need custom features that Shopify's plans and apps do not cover.
  • Costs: Shopify starts at $39 a month in the US ($29 billed yearly). WooCommerce is free, but WooCommerce.com estimates hosting at $25 to $350 a month for most stores.
  • Card fees: both start at 2.9% + 30¢ per online card payment in the US on their built-in processors.

What Is the Difference Between Shopify and WooCommerce?

Shopify is a Canadian company whose platform hosts your online store on its own servers. You pay a subscription, choose a theme, add products and start selling. Shopify handles hosting, the checkout, software updates and payment security.

WooCommerce is a plugin that turns a WordPress website into a store. The software is free and open source, maintained by Woo, part of Automattic. You choose your own web host, install WordPress and WooCommerce, and you own every file and database row. That freedom comes with work: someone has to keep the site updated, backed up and secure.

A useful way to think about it: Shopify is like renting a fully serviced shop unit, and WooCommerce is like owning the building. Owning is often cheaper and more flexible, but repairs are your problem.

Shopify vs WooCommerce Pricing: What You Will Really Pay

Shopify plans

Shopify's US plans are Basic at $39 a month, Grow at $105 and Advanced at $399 when billed monthly. Billed yearly they work out to $29, $79 and $299 a month, as listed in Website Builder Expert's March 2026 comparison. Shopify Plus, for large merchants, is reported to start at about $2,300 a month on a three-year term. Shopify's pricing page shows prices in your local currency, so check it from your own country before you sign up.

On top of the plan, many Shopify stores pay for apps (reviews, subscriptions, upsells and so on), which are billed monthly by their developers. Premium themes are a one-off cost.

WooCommerce costs

WooCommerce's own pricing page describes the core plugin as "Free, with no monthly subscription" and puts the other costs at:

  • Hosting: "$25 – $350/month for most stores, but scales with traffic and performance needs."
  • Extensions: "$29 – $299/year per extension."
  • Revenue share: "0% revenue share."

Add a domain name (usually $10 to $20 a year), an optional premium theme and, for many stores, a developer's time. The software is free; running it well is not.

Transaction Fees: Where the Real Money Goes

Card processing is the biggest ongoing cost on either platform, and on the built-in options the starting rate is the same.

  • Shopify Payments charges 2.9% + 30¢ per online card payment on the Basic plan in the US, falling to 2.7% + 30¢ on Grow. Higher plans get lower rates.
  • WooPayments charges 2.9% + 30¢ for US domestic cards, plus 1.5% for international cards and 1% when currency conversion applies, according to WooCommerce's fee schedule. In-person payments cost 2.7% + 10¢, plus $5 a month for each active card reader.

The key difference is what happens if you use another payment provider. WooCommerce charges nothing extra. Shopify adds its own fee on every order when you use a third-party provider instead of Shopify Payments: 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced and 0.2% on Plus, according to its pricing page. That fee is on top of whatever the other provider charges.

Ease of Use and Setup

Shopify is faster to launch. You sign up, pick a theme and add products from one dashboard, and there is no software to install. Website Builder Expert's testing, based on more than 300 hours of research, rated Shopify ahead for setup, design, security and support.

WooCommerce takes more steps: buy hosting, install WordPress, install WooCommerce, choose and configure a theme, then add payment, shipping and tax extensions. Many hosts offer one-click WooCommerce installs, which helps. If you already know WordPress, the learning curve is much smaller.

Customization and Control

WooCommerce wins on control. Because it is open source and self-hosted, you can change any part of the store, move to any host, and export all of your data whenever you like. That makes it a good fit for unusual products, complex pricing rules, membership sites and businesses where content and commerce sit side by side.

Shopify is flexible through themes, its app store and its developer tools, but you work inside Shopify's rules. Some deeper changes, such as fully customizing the checkout, are reserved for Shopify Plus.

Security and PCI Compliance

This is the difference most small businesses underestimate. Any store that accepts card payments has to meet the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS).

  • Shopify says it is "certified Level 1 PCI DSS compliant" and that this "extends by default to all stores powered by Shopify" (Shopify security page).
  • WooCommerce says "PCI DSS compliance is ultimately the responsibility of the store owner" and that the core plugin "is not PCI certified" (WooCommerce PCI guide). Using a hosted payment gateway reduces your exposure, but you still own the site's security.

For a WooCommerce store, that means keeping WordPress, WooCommerce, your theme and every plugin up to date, using a reputable host, and taking regular backups. Badly maintained shopping carts are easy for attackers to find, as our guide to the "ext:asp intitle:cart" search shows.

Selling in Person, on Social and Internationally

Shopify has its own point-of-sale (POS) system, so in-store and online stock, orders and customers live in one place. Its plans also include multichannel selling to marketplaces and social platforms, with sales tracked in the same dashboard.

WooCommerce supports in-person card payments through WooPayments card readers and third-party POS plugins, and it can sell through marketplaces and social channels using extensions. It works, but you assemble and maintain the pieces yourself.

For selling abroad, both handle multiple currencies and languages. On WooPayments, watch the 1.5% international card fee and 1% conversion fee. Shopify's international tools are most complete on its higher plans.

The Business Picture: Shopify's Growth and WooCommerce's Reach

Shopify is growing fast. In its third-quarter 2026 results, filed with the SEC on November 3, 2026, the company reported:

  • Gross merchandise volume (GMV, the total value of goods sold through its merchants' stores) of $119.1 billion, up 29% from a year earlier.
  • Revenue of $3.74 billion, up 32%.
  • Subscription revenue of $836 million, up 20%, and monthly recurring revenue of $230 million, up from $193 million.
  • Fourth-quarter guidance for revenue growth "at a low-thirties percentage rate".

Shopify makes most of its money from merchant services such as payments, not subscriptions: merchant solutions brought in $2.9 billion of the quarter's revenue. That is why the platform charges the extra fee when stores use another payment provider.

WooCommerce, by contrast, is used on more websites. W3Techs found it on 7.7% of all websites in November 2026, against 5.3% for Shopify. Many of those are small stores and side projects, which fits WooCommerce's free, do-it-yourself model.

Whichever you choose, plan your cash flow around card fees and stock, not the platform subscription. Can You Switch From WooCommerce to Shopify, or Back?

Yes. Both platforms let you export products, customers and orders as CSV files, and migration apps and agencies can move larger stores. Plan for some manual work: themes, custom features, URL structures and SEO redirects do not transfer automatically. Set up 301 redirects from old product URLs to new ones so you keep your search rankings.

Shopify vs WooCommerce FAQs

Is WooCommerce really free?

The plugin is free. You still pay for hosting (WooCommerce estimates $25 to $350 a month for most stores), a domain, card processing and any paid extensions or themes.

Is Shopify better than WooCommerce for beginners?

Usually yes. Shopify handles hosting, security and updates, and setup takes place in one dashboard. WooCommerce is easier only if you already know WordPress.

Which is cheaper, Shopify or WooCommerce?

For small stores using built-in payments, total costs are often similar because card fees dominate. WooCommerce becomes cheaper when you use a third-party payment provider, because Shopify adds 0.2% to 2% per order in that case.

Does Shopify charge transaction fees?

Not if you use Shopify Payments; you pay only the card rate. If you use another provider, Shopify charges 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced and 0.2% on Plus.

Is WooCommerce good for SEO?

Both platforms can rank well. WooCommerce runs on WordPress, which gives you full control over URLs, content and technical SEO. Shopify covers the basics well but has some fixed URL structures, such as /products/ and /collections/.

Which is more secure?

Shopify, for most small businesses, because it is PCI DSS Level 1 certified and maintains the platform for you. A WooCommerce store can be just as secure, but only if it is kept updated and well hosted.
