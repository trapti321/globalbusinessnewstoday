Lam Research Approves $25 Billion Buyback as Chip Stocks Swing

Lam Research approved a buyback of up to $25 billion and a $0.33 dividend on Nov 5, 2026, the day chip stocks plunged. What it means and how buybacks work.

GBNT Newsroom • Published Nov 9, 2026 • 4 min read
Share

Lam Research has approved a new share buyback of up to $25 billion and declared a quarterly dividend of $0.33 a share. The chip equipment maker announced the plan on November 5, 2026, the day semiconductor stocks plunged, and its shares jumped about 11% before the market opened the next morning.

Key facts:

  • Buyback: up to $25 billion, added to what remains of the previous authorization
  • Time limit: none, and Lam is not required to buy any set amount
  • Dividend: $0.33 a share, payable January 6, 2027, to shareholders on record on December 2, 2026
  • Announced: November 5, 2026

What Lam Research Announced

According to the company's press release filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, Lam's board authorised the repurchase of up to $25 billion of common stock. The new authorization is in addition to the balance left on its previous program, which the release did not quantify.

Buybacks can be made in the open market or through private deals, and may use structured agreements. The program has no end date and can be suspended at any time.

Chief financial officer Doug Bettinger said the authorization "reinforces our capital allocation plan and commitment to return significant value to stockholders," pointing to the company's "robust cash generation".

Why the Timing Matters

The announcement came on a brutal day for chip stocks. On November 5 the PHLX Semiconductor Index fell about 12% and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.5% into correction. Investors feared that the Democratic midterm wins could slow the construction of the data centers that drive demand for chips and chipmaking tools.

A large buyback signals that management sees value in its own shares after a sell-off. Lam stock rose nearly 11% before the market opened on November 6 as chip stocks rebounded. For the wider picture, see our reports on the Nasdaq's correction and rebound and on why Meta says data centers will be harder to build.

What Lam Research Does

Lam Research makes the equipment used to manufacture semiconductors, particularly machines for etching and depositing the ultra-thin layers that make up a chip. Its customers are chipmakers such as memory and logic producers. When AI spending lifts demand for advanced chips, those manufacturers buy more equipment. That makes Lam sensitive to any slowdown in data center investment.

How Share Buybacks Work

  • Fewer shares: when a company buys back its own stock, the number of shares in circulation falls, so each remaining share represents a bigger slice of profits.
  • Not a promise: an authorization sets a maximum. Lam says it is not obliged to buy any particular amount.
  • A use of cash: money spent on buybacks is not spent on research, factories or acquisitions, so investors weigh it against growth plans.

Lam Research Buyback FAQs

How big is Lam Research's new buyback?

Up to $25 billion of common stock, on top of the remaining balance of its previous authorization.

When is Lam's next dividend?

$0.33 a share, payable on January 6, 2027, to shareholders of record on December 2, 2026.

Why did Lam Research stock rise?

The buyback announcement coincided with a rebound in chip stocks on November 6 after a steep sell-off the previous day.
